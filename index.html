Agentic AI for Trustworthy Engineering Design

Agentic AI refers to AI systems that can interpret high-level objectives, plan and coordinate multi-step tasks, use specialized computational tools, and verify intermediate results with limited human intervention. Engineering design is the process of translating system requirements and constraints into a functioning product—a process complicated by nonlinear physics coupling, expensive simulations, uncertainty, and limited derivative information. The main goal of this project is to develop agentic AI methods that drive engineering design by connecting disparate physics models, learning efficiently from limited simulation data, quantifying uncertainty, and guiding design optimization. Trustworthiness will be established through computational verification and direct comparison with physical evidence. The methods will be benchmarked using PEARL, a solar-powered floating platform for recharging autonomous underwater vehicles and relaying their data. Predictions will be evaluated against measurements from a scale-model PEARL prototype through initial pool testing and controlled tow-tank experiments involving representative wave conditions, towing speeds, and docking maneuvers.

Research

The project brings together uncertainty-aware data-driven AI/ML methods, agentic AI, and physical prototyping and validation. We will develop novel computational algorithms that enable design and decision-making under uncertainty, agentic AI frameworks that automate the interfacing between physics models and our algorithms, and physical prototyping for design verification and validation. Measurements from physical prototyping will inform improvements in the computational algorithms forming a closed loop.

Data-driven algorithms

Complex systems can require thousands of simulations before their parts work together as intended. The team will develop models that learn from carefully selected simulations, show where predictions are uncertain, and focus each new calculation where it is most useful. These methods will help engineers explore and improve designs while reducing computing time and cost.

Physical prototyping

PEARL is a solar-powered floating station designed to recharge autonomous underwater vehicles and send their data by satellite. The project will study its shape, stability, power, propulsion, communications, and cost while accounting for changing ocean conditions. The team will build a scale model and compare computer predictions with measurements from pool and tow-tank tests.

Agentic AI

Engineering teams often use separate software programs for each part of a system, making them difficult and time-consuming to connect. The project will create an AI assistant that turns a user's written natural language instructions into a connected workflow. Built-in checks will confirm that units, data sizes, and transferred information agree before costly simulations begin.
